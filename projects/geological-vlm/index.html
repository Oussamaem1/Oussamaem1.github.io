Vision–Language Models for Geological Interpretation

Testing whether a small vision–language model, adapted on domain imagery, can take on several geological interpretation tasks at once instead of one model per task. Facies identification was handled separately with spectral methods — SAM, SID and NMF unmixing.

Context
Cleverlytics — for a major mining group
Period
Oct. 2025 — present
Location
Morocco

Disclosure

Client-confidential engagement. The imagery and the client are not disclosable. The two F1 scores below are the figures published on my CV; results for the remaining tasks are not disclosed.

Problem

Geological interpretation of core and face imagery is manual and depends on expert availability. The question was whether a compact vision–language model could be adapted to carry out several of those interpretation tasks directly from images.

Constraints

The model had to be small enough to fine-tune and run economically, which ruled out large multimodal models and pushed towards parameter-efficient adaptation on a modest domain dataset.

My contribution

I ran the fine-tuning and evaluation work: task framing, the QLoRA adaptation of Qwen2-VL-2B across the five tasks, per-task evaluation, and the spectral approach used for facies.

Delivered within the Cleverlytics data science team; the contributions described here are my own.

Approach

  • Fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-2B with 4-bit QLoRA across five geological interpretation tasks.
  • Evaluated each task separately with F1 rather than reporting a single aggregate score.
  • Treated facies identification as a spectral problem instead, using Spectral Angle Mapper, Spectral Information Divergence and NMF unmixing.

Tools

  • PyTorch
  • Qwen2-VL-2B
  • QLoRA / PEFT
  • SAM / SID
  • NMF
  • Python

Technical domains

Data

Client-owned geological imagery and spectral data. Not publishable.

Results

  • F1 = 94.5% on layer-transition identification.
  • F1 = 98.7% on sterility-level classification.

Limitations

  • The published figures cover two of the five tasks; results for the others are not disclosable.
  • Evaluation is on the client own data distribution and should not be read as a general geological benchmark.
