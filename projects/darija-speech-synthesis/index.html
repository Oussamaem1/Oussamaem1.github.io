Darija Speech Synthesis for a Voice Assistant

Giving a banking voice assistant a natural-sounding Moroccan Darija voice, working from a small corpus and a language with no standard written form. Evaluation was made objective by transcribing the synthesized speech with Whisper large-v3 and measuring the error.

Context
Cleverlytics — banking sector
Period
Oct. 2025 — present
Location
Morocco

Disclosure

Client-confidential engagement. The client is not named and no audio samples are published here.

Problem

Moroccan Darija has no standard orthography and very little public speech data, which makes an off-the-shelf text-to-speech model a poor fit for a Darija banking assistant.

Constraints

The available corpus was small — 1,711 utterances from 7 speakers — and users write Darija in Latin script (Arabizi) as often as in Arabic script, so the front end had to accept both kinds of input.

My contribution

I carried out the model adaptation and the evaluation design: the StyleTTS 2 adaptation, the aligner change, the Arabizi grapheme-to-phoneme front end, and the ASR-based benchmark.

Delivered within the Cleverlytics data science team; the contributions described here are my own.

Approach

  • Adapted StyleTTS 2 on an ary_Arab corpus of 1,711 utterances from 7 speakers.
  • Unfroze the aligner so it could learn Arabic alignment rather than inherit a mismatched one.
  • Built a dedicated Arabizi to IPA grapheme-to-phoneme front end for Latin-script Darija input.
  • Measured quality objectively by transcribing synthesized speech with Whisper large-v3 and scoring transcription error on a standardized benchmark.

Tools

  • PyTorch
  • StyleTTS 2
  • Whisper large-v3
  • Arabizi → IPA G2P
  • Python

Technical domains

Generative & Multimodal AIprimary

Data

An ary_Arab Darija speech corpus of 1,711 utterances from 7 speakers, used under the client engagement.

Limitations

  • A 1,711-utterance corpus from 7 speakers bounds speaker variety and phonetic coverage.
  • ASR-based scoring measures intelligibility well, but it is not a substitute for human listening tests on naturalness.
  • Benchmark scores from this engagement are not disclosable.
